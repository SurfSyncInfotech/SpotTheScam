Quick Reference

Three Core Safety Rules

Keep these three simple habits in mind whenever you read an unexpected email, text message, or notification.

1

Stop — Don't rush under pressure

Scammers almost always try to make you panic. They use phrases like "Account suspended in 24 hours!", "Urgent payment needed", or "Claim your prize before midnight".

Real-life tip: Legitimate companies, schools, and banks do not delete your account or demand money in a frantic countdown. Take a breath before clicking anything.

2

Check — Look past the display name

Anyone can name their email "Netflix Support" or "School IT Department". What matters is the actual email address inside the angle brackets (e.g., support@netfIix-security.com instead of netflix.com).

Real-life tip: Watch out for sneaky letter swaps like a lowercase L replaced with an uppercase I, or extra words added to the domain name.

3

Verify — Use a separate trusted path

If an email says your password expired or your package is delayed, do not click the link or call the phone number inside that email. That link leads straight to the scammer's fake page.

Real-life tip: Open a fresh browser tab, type in the real website address yourself (or open the official app), and check your account from there.

What should you do if you clicked a suspicious link?

  1. Do not enter any passwords or codes: If a page asks for your password, PIN, or verification code, close the tab immediately.
  2. Tell a trusted adult, parent, or teacher right away: Scams happen to everyone. Letting someone know early prevents any damage.
  3. Change your password from the real website: Go to the official service (e.g., Google, your school portal) from a clean tab and update your password.
